# Jobs

A job is one piece of work for one customer: what it is, who it is for, what it
is printing on, what it will cost and what you are charging.

## Taking a job

**Job → New job**, or ⌘N. You need a customer and a name for the work; the rest
can follow. Picking a model from your library fills in the material and the
weight, so the price is computed rather than guessed.

## Stages

Every job sits at a stage, and the sidebar counts them:

- **Pending** — accepted, not started.
- **Printing** — on a machine now.
- **Post** — off the machine, being finished: supports, sanding, painting.
- **QC** — being checked.
- **Completed** — done and ready.
- **Delivered** — in the customer's hands.

**Delivered comes after completed.** Several figures in this app count delivered
work as well as completed work, because a job that reached a customer is the one
that earned.

## Changing one

Select a job and use the **Job** menu, or right-click it in the table. From
there you can edit it, record a payment, put it on hold, mark it delivered,
raise an invoice, or record what a print actually took.

## What it actually took

When a print finishes you can record the real weight and the real time, rather
than the estimate. That is what turns the shop's figures from a quote into an
account: the material comes off the spool, and the difference between estimated
and actual is what the accuracy figures are built from.

## Searching

The search box at the top of the window narrows the jobs table by **project,
customer or job number** — the three things somebody at the bench has to hand.
