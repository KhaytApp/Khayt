# The catalogue

Not the library — that is the files. The catalogue is what you have decided to
**sell**: a product, with a price you stand behind.

## Adding a product

**Job → New product…**, or the **+** button on the catalogue. You need a name in
at least one language.

## One tab per language

The name and description have a tab for each language your catalogue carries,
set in Settings. A shop selling in German gets a German tab, and what you type
is stored under that language rather than being flattened into one field.

A tab with nothing written in it is marked with a dot, because a half-translated
catalogue is otherwise invisible: the storefront falls back to another language
and the row looks finished.

## The price says where it came from

Under every price is the reason for it:

- **Your own price** — you typed it.
- **Rounded from** — computed, then rounded, and the figure it was rounded from
  is shown.
- **Calculated** — from the parts, the material and your margin.

A price nobody can check is a price nobody should trust, which is why the reason
is always there.

## Margin

Leave it empty and the product takes your shop's default margin. **Empty is not
zero** — a product with a margin of zero is one you sell at cost.

## List or grid

The toggle in the toolbar switches between them. The list is the only view that
puts margin next to weight, which is what pricing work needs; the grid is for
recognising a thing by looking at it.

## What is edited in the other app

Parts, prices per quantity, photos and documents are kept in Khayt for Windows
and Linux. Editing a product here leaves all of them exactly as they were.
